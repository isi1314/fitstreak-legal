How to keep a workout streak

Most people don't quit because workouts are hard. They quit because one missed Tuesday turns into a missed week, and starting again feels like failing twice. A streak helps when it is built to survive a bad day.

1. Make the minimum embarrassingly small

Decide what the smallest workout that still counts is. Five minutes is enough to count. On a good day you'll do more; on a bad day the small version keeps the chain alive, and that is the whole point.

2. Tie it to something you already do

"After I make coffee" works better than "sometime today". Pick a moment that already happens every day and put the workout right after it.

3. Plan rest on purpose

Rest days are part of training, not a break in it. Decide which days are rest days before the week starts, so resting is keeping the plan rather than breaking it.

4. Decide now what happens when you miss

You will miss a day. Choose the rule in advance: the next day you do the five-minute version, no catching up, no double sessions. A missed day only ends a habit when it ends the plan.

5. Make it a little social, or a little silly

Accountability works even when it is gentle. A friend who asks, a note on the fridge, or a small character who is waiting for you all do the same job: someone notices.

6. Watch something grow

Counting days is motivating for a while. Seeing something change, like a number of workouts, a longer hold or a companion that grows up, keeps it interesting after the novelty wears off.
