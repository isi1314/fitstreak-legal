Low-impact workouts at home

"Low impact" means at least one foot stays on the ground and nothing lands hard. It is not the same as easy. Standing up from a chair twelve times without your hands is real leg work.

This guide sticks to two rules: nothing jumps, and nothing needs the floor. Everything below can be done in a small room with one sturdy chair.

Move slowly, keep the chair within reach, and stop if something hurts. If you have a condition that affects exercise, check with your doctor first.

Moves that do the work

Legs

  • Sit to stand. Stand up from the chair without using your hands, then sit back down slowly.
  • Chair-supported squats. Hold the chair back and lower your hips as if sitting, then stand.
  • Standing calf raises. Hold the chair for balance, rise onto your toes, lower slowly.

Upper body

  • Wall push-ups. Hands on the wall at shoulder height, bend your elbows and press back.
  • Seated overhead reach. Reach both arms overhead, then lower them slowly.

Balance

  • Heel-to-toe balance. Hold the chair and place one foot directly in front of the other, then hold.
  • Standing side steps. Step to one side and back again, staying near the chair.

Heart rate

  • Standing march. Hold the chair lightly and march in place, lifting each knee.
  • Seated toe taps. Tap your toes in front of you, alternating feet at a brisk pace.

Three or four short sessions a week is a fine start. Add a round when the last few reps stop feeling like work.
